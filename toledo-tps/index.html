How the 294 splits

Sean gave the total, not the per-school breakdown — “a lot of these are 25 or more.” The column above is an enrollment-weighted allocation of his 294, averaging 24.5 per school.

Treat it as a staging guide only. BJ's sheet holds the real per-school counts; get them Monday and this column is replaced, not adjusted.

The meal estimate survives

294 cases against 3,230 estimated meals across a four-component hamburger meal implies 44 servings per case — an ordinary K–12 pack.

The enrollment model and Sean's live count agree. What was wrong in the first pass was the assumed case pack, not the meal math.

Temperature changed

The 8/14 email said frozen. On the call Sean confirmed that for a Tuesday-night load the product has to ride refrigerated, not frozen — it is pulled to thaw for Thursday service.

Set the reefer to refrigerated, not 0°F, and log the pulp temp at every drop.

Equipment call

One 26′ reefer — and it is exactly full.

Twelve 48×40 pallets is every floor position in a 26′ box. Interior runs about 306″ by 96″, which takes pallets two across by six deep: 12 positions, 12 pallets, zero spare. The first pass called this a 7% cube load; that was cube, and cube was the wrong constraint. One-school-per-pallet makes floor positions binding.

Still one truck and one driver — you do not need the second big truck you flagged to Sean. But the slack is gone: there is no room for a 13th pallet, a pallet that arrives oversized, or a rebuild on the dock at 4 AM. At their own 56-case stack standard, 294 cases would ride on six pallets; the accuracy decision costs six positions and all of the margin.
