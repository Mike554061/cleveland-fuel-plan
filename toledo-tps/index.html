The rounding tax

Pooled district-wide, 3,230 meals is 96 cases. Split into 12 school-specific stacks with no partial cases, it becomes 118 cases.

That 22-case premium is the cost of 12 discrete drops instead of one bulk drop. Worth naming in the rate conversation with Sean.

How to build it

  • 12 segregated, shrink-wrapped, school-labeled stacks — not mixed pallets
  • Average 9.8 cases per school; only McTigue and Beverly exceed 10
  • Load in reverse stop order so stop 1 is at the door
  • Consolidate onto 4 pallets, 3 school stacks each, slip-sheeted — or 12 quarter-pallets if Atlantic ships that way

Equipment call

One 26′ reefer. One driver. No second truck.

At 118 cases the load is roughly 118 cu ft against about 1,600 cu ft of usable space in a 26′ reefer — 7% cube utilization, about a pallet and a half. The constraint on this run is not capacity, it is the 4 hours of stop time and the 240 miles of deadhead.

Your 8/21 note to Sean flagged needing “another truck, another big truck, and a really good delivery driver — all within the next 48 hours.” The math says the truck is not the problem. The driver is. Twelve POD-required school drops in an unfamiliar city on a 10.4-hour day is the entire risk in this run, and that is where the next 48 hours should go.
