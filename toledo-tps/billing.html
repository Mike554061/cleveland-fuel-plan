The $20 stop is the leak

At $86/hr a twenty-minute stop is worth $28.67 of truck time. Billing $20 for it gives back $8.67 a stop — and this run has twelve of them.

You are paying for four hours you do not bill

The driver is on the clock 10.38 hours. You bill 6.38. The four hours of stop time are wage, fuel and insurance you carry, compensated by a flat $240.

Cleveland to Toledo makes this worse than a local route: the deadhead is already 4.8 billable hours, so the stop time is pure added exposure on an already long day.

Method. Controllables are costs that move with the run; fixed costs are day-allocated. Contribution margin is net sales less controllables — the number that tells you whether a marginal load is worth taking. Operating profit is after fixed allocation. Driver wages are charged on on-duty hours — load-out, drive and stop time in one line — so load-out labour is not double counted. Wage, burden, insurance, lease, admin and maintenance reserve are planning defaults, not your books; replace them with actuals and every figure updates.
