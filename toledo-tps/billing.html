It makes money — just the least of any Toledo run

Two stops earns $91.55 on a 6.18-hour day. The same truck on the 14-stop run earns $347.57 on 11.31 hours.

Per billable hour that is $14.81 against $30.74. Same road, same rate — twice the margin per hour.

Why stops are the cheap hours

Transit hours burn fuel, tires and wages. Stop hours burn wages and a few minutes of reefer, and almost no miles.

So cost per billable hour falls as stops go up: $71.19 at two stops, $55.26 at fourteen. The rate never moves, so every point of that is margin.

Where SLA earns most

Wednesday 8/26 SLA rides with the schools. Those two stops add 0.67 h and 2.7 mi to a truck already going — roughly $58 charged against about $21 of cost.

Run alone the same two stops carry the whole road themselves. Attached to a school run they are close to pure margin.

The rule

Every stop you add to a Toledo truck is worth about $19 of margin

An extra stop charges $28.67 of clock and costs roughly $10 in wages and a mile or two of running. Nothing else on the run changes. That is the whole case for loading the truck up rather than sending it out light.
